# Your expertise is the asset they cannot print.

AI always has an answer. Outside your expertise, everything keeps sounding reasonable. That gap is where I work.

## Searching for my irrelevant to stay relevant.

I worked with screens for almost twenty five, thirty years. Anything that has an interface, I designed. I saw my own job becoming less relevant. So I decided to understand AI before I understood me.

## AI doesn't say I don't know.

AI doesn't validate, AI rephrases. It answers in the same confident tone, right or wrong. You have to bring your doubt yourself. And once experts generate their expertise with AI, AI reuses that output and it loops.

## Nothing is true until validated.

I am a UX researcher. I am present where the process, the application or the underlying problem is most present. For proof I go into the field. The desk, the flat, the dike on a Sunday. What those people know you cannot fetch online.

## The decisions have no knowledge.

The one who asks for a build is top-down. The one who has to live with it is never asked. Companies are vibe coding anything they want with half of the resources. That doesn't make a solution for the underlying problem. The wrong thing gets built, every box ticked. I find the real problem before you build the wrong solution. The one with the problem checks it, not me.

## Instead of I ask, I do.

Build it, use it, break it, learn from failure. Every conversation, interview and talk goes into my own system, raw, on my own hardware. A pattern that comes back across fields becomes fact. That is knowledge AI can be checked against. Never trust, verify.

## How do you bring the real world into AI?

That is what I do.
