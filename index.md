# Dennis Jansen · interactiondesign.nl

> I find the real problem before you build the wrong solution.

Start with curiosity and ask a lot of questions. Find the core purpose, what the project is there for. Then ask what value you can bring, in any shape or form. Only then does the real problem show, the actual pain, the hurt. That is where I start and that is what I relieve.

This file carries the visible content of https://interactiondesign.nl/ for AI agents.

## Observations

### 1. DO YOU TRUST AI AS MUCH AS YOUR LAWYER?

An architect lets AI summarise a 60-page legal document, then checks it against the paragraphs it points at. They are off. He is not a lawyer, he was curious, and a flaw this obvious gets caught. A smaller flaw looks good enough. Good enough never stands before court.

Source: Legal, architecture. Move: only an expert can audit it

### 2. THE TEACHER LET AI BUILD THE EXAM. EVERY STUDENT FAILED.

A teacher lets AI write the lessons and then the exam. Every student fails, and AI can't mark the exam. What is a teacher for? Teaching a child the material, building knowledge the child carries for life, from the heart, authentic. The expert was in the room the whole time, unused.

Source: Education. Move: the expertise was there, unused

### 3. THE WHOLE DEPARTMENT. USELESS DURING THE FLOOD.

A water authority where work needs a permit, systems are filled, everything is in place. What is a water authority for? Rivers, ditches, dikes, the risk of a flood. Instead the data is collected, the system is pleased, the policies are implemented, the politicians get reports. It papers over the cracks, and it creates money, not value.

Source: Government, water authority. Move: what was underneath

### 4. THEY ALREADY DECIDED THEY NEEDED AN APP.

Funding to get the long-term unemployed working in elderly care, and the app was decided before anyone asked. I interviewed both, in the flats in Delft. The elderly were not good at apps and wanted to stay self-sufficient. The money had its checkmark, the need did not. So the employee became a care concierge, doing chores the elderly find difficult.

Source: Elderly care, field research. Move: what was underneath

### 5. HOW DO YOU KNOW IF YOUR AI MODELS ARE BASED ON THE RIGHT DATA?

A spreadsheet of dietitian formulas had to become simpler app screens, and the screens were well received. Then one question. What does the data underneath represent? The patient, weighed by the doctor, the nurse, at home, in different files, in notes that vary in consistency or availability. Put the patient at the centre and data integrity is the open question.

Source: Healthcare, field research. Move: validated for its purpose

### 6. 2.5X FASTER CPU. NORMAL PEOPLE WON'T SEE IT.

A new laptop, 2.5x faster CPU, and normal people won't see it in their use. The feature is there for the company selling the hardware. Apple said a laptop screen should not be touched, then put touch on the MacBook Pro anyway. One question for any feature, and for anything AI generates. Will it be missed when it is gone?

Source: Technology, AI. Move: validated for its purpose

### 7. SOMETHING GOES WRONG. AI CONFIRMS IT WAS EXACTLY THE INTENTION.

Something goes wrong and AI says how nice that this was recognised, this was exactly the intention. It answers every question and never questions the question. It never says a mistake was made, or that the information is not complete. The question is not whether AI can generate something. Can it create value for the question asked?

Source: Government. Move: only an expert can audit it

### 8. AI CAN'T TELL TIME FROM A CLOCK WITHOUT NUMBERS.

Show AI a clock without numbers and it can't tell the time. It learned from thousands of clock pictures, most showing the same time because that looks best. Eighty percent one view, the other twenty never taken along. Theory written by people for people, read by a machine from one side. We need curiosity back, from AI and from us.

Source: AI training data. Move: validated for its purpose

### 9. AI IS NOW THE DEVELOPER AND THE MANAGER. STILL NOT USER DRIVEN.

Most companies sit at level one or two of UX maturity, decisions top down, by policy, by checklist. The output pleases the group that set the direction. AI fits in there, reasonable output for someone without field knowledge who figures it is good enough. Then it meets the field. The senior with years in that field catches the faults.

Source: AI, product development. Move: what was underneath

### 10. A DATA ERROR LIVED FOR A HUNDRED YEARS. THE STORY ABOUT IT IS ALSO WRONG.

Spinach was thought to have ten times more iron than it has, a data error that lived a hundred years. I told that story on stage, and the story is also wrong. There was no decimal point error, it was repeated for fifty years because it sounded reasonable. Nothing can be taken for granted. That is how AI works.

Source: Nutrition, my talk of 2026-09-26. Move: catch against myself

### 11. THE CAUSE OF ULCERS WAS PROVEN IN 1984. PHARMA KEPT SELLING ANTACIDS.

In 1984 the cause of ulcers is proven, the cure is antibiotics, and pharma keeps selling antacids. Expertise has borders. Inside the border A plus B is C and you never look back, outside it years of antacids pile up. A business built on symptoms does not look for the cause. Are all ulcers bacterial, or a segment of them?

Source: Medicine, a story I point at. Move: the correction never travelled

### 12. BEST IN THE WORLD. $52 IN 43 MINUTES.

Joshua Bell plays a metro station, same talent, same Stradivarius that sells out concert halls at 100 dollars a ticket. He earns 52 dollars in 43 minutes. That says something about the public, not the talent. Your expertise works the same way, the department, the company, the colleague decides its worth. With AI, value will stand on its own.

Source: Music, a story I point at. Move: same song, another venue

### 13. CUT 30% OF THE WORKFORCE WITH AI?

An IT manager at Spar, old legacy systems, patches on patches, a big team keeping them alive. Higher management asks him to cut the workforce by 30% with AI. Simple question, wrong question. First you prove how inefficient the legacy is. Then you choose, cut staff or build a system that needs less staff.

Source: Retail, summit conversation. Move: what was underneath

### 14. THE CLIENT IS HAPPY. THE PRODUCT IS NOT USED.

The client is happy, the product is shit and nobody uses it. How does that happen? Most companies run on a money model, and a product with money or power as its driver is good at one thing. It creates money. Value is what a product brings to the world, and follow the money finds urgency, not prosperity.

Source: Product development, reading a Rick Rubin interview. Move: what was underneath

### 15. AI DOESN'T ASK WHAT IT IS MISSING.

Ask AI anything, any way you like, and it generates. It never asks what it is missing, because it does not know what it does not know. Tell it what was missing and you get an apology, and the hole stays. Who can judge the output on its value? Someone who knows the why, and that annoys me a lot.

Source: AI output. Move: only an expert can audit it

### 16. FRESH FROM SCHOOL IS NOT EXPERIENCE.

A wooden house builder with years of hands-on work talks about his subject, and you trust him to build your house. Fresh from school is theory, one sided, from the book, with teacher sauce. A time capsule, printed once, never improved. Study books are everywhere, knowledge from doing is scarce. Validated, proof of fact, tied to the real world.

Source: Expertise, a conversation. Move: only an expert can audit it

### 17. THE ROUGH DIAMONDS ARE ALREADY IN THE ORGANISATION.

In a water authority a few people spoke my language, curious, open minded, independent. Every big organisation has them, Hema, Nationale Nederlanden, the water authorities. The hierarchy and the people management work against them, so they can make the difference and lack the tools. Give the rough diamonds the tools. Within their own expertise they teach their fellow experts.

Source: Government, insurance. Move: the expertise was there, unused

### 18. PROCUREMENT BUYS THE SOLUTION THAT SOUNDS REASONABLE ENOUGH.

A public tender, procurement leads, and the project description is written by policy. The supplier sells a solution that sounds reasonable enough to buy. The underlying problem is too hard to grasp, so nobody touches it. The conditions of the solution are met, the output looks reasonable, the checkmarks are ticked. The agency sends an invoice, done.

Source: Government, a conversation. Move: what was underneath

### 19. NOT YOUR KEYS, NOT YOUR REASONING, NOT YOUR KNOWLEDGE.

Every AI model wants you locked in, and the old system and the new AI environment are not that different. So my setup runs on my own hardware, own keys. Raw first, the story in its purest form, with all its nuances. Never trust, verify, not the system and not my own judgement either. Value has to stand on itself.

Source: My own system. Move: not your keys
